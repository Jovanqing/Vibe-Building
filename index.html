Physics-Engine-in-the-Loop

Vibe Building

A deterministic physics engine, not a language model, decides whether a building design is accepted.

Abstract

Automated building design must comply with seismic and wind codes and satisfy structural mechanics constraints, yet most existing agents produce visually plausible models without verification grounded in mechanical analysis and code compliance. We introduce the Vibe Building task and propose PE-Loop (Physics-Engine-in-the-Loop), an agent in which a deterministic physics engine is the sole source of evaluation signals, mapping code constraints to a physics process reward, while the language model is confined to proposing discrete revisions (section menu, topology, and lateral system). Designs are verified by held-out seismic and wind time-history checks and a constructability gate. On VB-Bench, 3,577 physics-adjudicated building instances across six code families, PE-Loop achieves the highest verified success rate under three of four backbone LLMs, the highest held-out seismic pass rate under all four, and the highest held-out wind pass rate under three. Replacing the physics verdict with a language-model judge, all else fixed, leaves 58.43% of accepted designs noncompliant. These results suggest that reliable structural design rests less on a stronger LLM proposer than on an adjudicator the proposer cannot influence, a division of labor for agents whose outputs must hold up in the physical world.

The task

An instance pairs an observation with a site configuration. The observation may be a textual brief, a hand-drawn sketch, or a set of site photographs. The design must satisfy the code envelope for that site, and it is carried to a certified optimum rather than to a first feasible guess.

Method

The language model proposes discrete moves and pays nothing for them. The physics engine decides, and every decision costs one evaluation of the budget. The loop terminates only when every strength and serviceability constraint holds.

  1. B0–B2 language model

    Perception and intent

    One vision-language call turns a sketch or an image into a massing. A second call fixes the intent (the lateral system, the governing load, and the strategy) and with it the initial discrete state. Neither call is charged to the evaluation budget.

  2. C1 language model

    One discrete repair per round

    Given only the set of violated constraints, the model picks one action: adjust the section triple, add a core or a shear wall, change the core plan, or change the lateral system. Three rounds, one lever level each.

  3. C2 physics engine

    The minimal feasible column

    With the discrete state fixed, the column side is searched on a 45-value, 50 mm grid by an upper-bound probe, six bisection steps, and a confirmation. This costs at most 9 oracle calls per round, and the first passing side is also the least-material one.

  4. C3 physics engine

    Material loop, ultra tier only

    After a pass, a finite menu along the section triple is enumerated and a candidate is accepted only if it is still feasible and cheaper. The final design is re-measured, and the held-out analyses and the certificate are computed offline.

Results

Swap the backbone, keep the reliability

Because the verdict never depends on the proposer, the held-out pass rate barely moves when the language model behind it changes. A baseline that judges itself inherits the model’s variance instead.

Where certified success is won

Raw compliance and certified success are not the same ranking. Designs that pass in the loop still fall at the constructability gate and at the held-out dynamic checks, and systems reorder once those gates are applied.

Table 5. The margin oracle claims exactly what it delivers. The language-model judge claims 49.44% and delivers 20.56%. The oracle’s two zeros are zero by construction, not for want of data: its gate is the physics check itself, so what it reports and what verification confirms are the same quantity, leaving no false acceptance and no evaluation spent on a misdirected repair.

Constructability costs raw compliance and pays certified success

With the constructability projection, every statically compliant design satisfies the detailing clause by construction, so the detailing gap closes to zero. Raw PASS drops by 29.74 pp and certified success rises by 9.74 pp.

What the language model actually buys

On the 59-instance reference set that pure search already solves, PE-Loop ties LLM-free search on feasibility at 98.31% with no discordant instances, so the language model buys no feasibility. What it buys is budget (55.86% NFE@pass against 97.78–100.00%) and held-out generalization (72.41% wind against 18.97–31.03%). The price is material: 5.66% more concrete than pure search.

What the designs are checked against

Verification runs against held-out record libraries, not against the loads the design was tuned on: forty-four far-field ground motions and sixteen ten-minute wind series, eight recorded in the field and eight synthesised to match the Kaimal and Davenport targets.
