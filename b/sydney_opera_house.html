# Sydney Opera House — roof shells in OpenSeesPy

**This is a demonstration, not a validation.** It exists to show that the
build → analyse → render toolchain generalises past rectangular towers to a
doubly-curved shell roof. No measured dynamic data for the Sydney Opera House
exists in the public record — no ambient-vibration survey, no forced-vibration
test, no instrumented record has been published. There is therefore **nothing
to compare the computed periods against, and no validation is possible or
claimed**. Every period quoted below is indicative only. Nothing in this
document should be read as agreement with, or a prediction of, the real
building's behaviour.

Build: `build_model.py`. Numbers: `results.json`. Renders: `paper/`,
`studio/`.

## The one result worth leading with

Two published numbers — the longest rib's **64 m arc** on a **75 m sphere**, and
its **54.6 m rise** above the springing — fix the chord at
`2R sin(θ/2) = 62.06 m` and therefore fix the horizontal offset of the rib tip
at `√(62.06² − 54.6²) = 29.53 m`. That tip *is* the crest, and the crest lies on
the hall's central plane, so the two pedestals of the largest shell are
**59.07 m apart**. Nothing was chosen. Two halls side by side at that span come
to **118 m** — against a published podium width of **120 m**, a number that was
never fed into the derivation. The geometry closes on itself.

The same construction puts every rib node on the 75 m sphere to
**1.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ m**, and the highest roof point at 67 m ASL minus the 54.6 m rise
puts the springing at **12.4 m ASL**, which is where the podium deck sits.

## 2. The geometry, derived not drawn

No node is hand-placed. One half-shell is generated from four numbers: the
sphere radius `R = 75 m`, the rib spacing `3.65°`, the longest rib arc `64 m`
and the crest height `54.6 m`.

Put the sphere's **pole at the pedestal** — that is what "the ribs coalesce at
their common springing point" and "great circles through the pole" mean
together. Let `n̂` be the inward normal at the pole and `t̂(φ)` the tangent of
the meridian at longitude `φ`, `φ = 0` being the rib in the hall's central
plane. Every point of the shell is then

```
X(θ, φ) = P + R sin θ · t̂(φ) + R (1 − cos θ) · n̂
```

with `θ` the arc angle from the pedestal, so `arc = R θ` exactly. `|X − C| = R`
for every `θ, φ`, where `C = P + R n̂` — this is the identity the third
verification assertion checks numerically.

**Derived, in order:**

1. **Pedestal tangent tilt `α`.** The longest rib has arc 64 m, i.e.
   `θ = 64/75 = 0.8533 rad = 48.89°`, and must rise 54.6 m. Solving
   `R sin θ cos α − R(1 − cos θ) sin α = 54.6` gives **α = 3.963°**. The ribs
   leave the pedestal almost vertically and lean 3.96° over the hall.
2. **Pedestal half-span `d`.** The chord of a 64 m arc on a 75 m sphere is
   `2R sin(θ/2) = 62.06 m`; with a rise of 54.6 m the horizontal offset of the
   rib tip is `√(62.06² − 54.6²) = 29.53 m`. The rib tip *is* the crest and the
   crest lies on the hall's central plane, so **the pedestals of the largest
   shell are 2 × 29.53 = 59.07 m apart** — forced, not chosen. Two halls side
   by side at that span come to 118 m, which is why the published podium is
   120 m wide. That agreement was not imposed; it fell out.
3. **The ridge.** The two mirror half-shells are cut from two 75 m spheres
   mirrored about the hall's central plane, so their intersection — the ridge —
   is a **circular arc of radius 59.78 m** in that plane, apex 54.6 m above the
   springing. Every rib runs from the pedestal until it meets that plane; that
   is the main shells' boundary rule, and it is why no rib length had to be
   invented.
4. **The shell family.** All ten shells are cut from the same 75 m sphere, so a
   shell is fully specified by its pedestal half-span `d`; crest height and rib
   length follow. Small shells are automatically narrower *and* shorter, as the
   real ones are.
5. **Side shells** use the other published boundary rule: a **small circle**
   (constant `θ`), giving the low broad crescents D5–D8.
6. **Springing level.** Highest roof point 67 m ASL minus crest 54.6 m above
   springing ⇒ **podium top at 12.4 m ASL**. The podium deck sits there.
7. **Segment length.** 64 m / 13 = **4.923 m** per precast rib segment. Every
   other rib is divided into `round(arc / 4.923)` equal-arc segments, minimum 4.

**Emergent, not imposed:** the model contains **2524 rib segments**
(`rib_major + rib_minor + rib_rest + pedestal` in `results.json`) against a
published "over 2400", and 2392 tile-lid courses each standing for two lids,
i.e. ≈ 4784 lids against a published 4253. The fan extents that produce these
numbers were fitted by eye, so this is a sanity check, not a verification.

## 4. Assumed, because it is not published in usable form

**Rib sections.** A line element cannot carry a solid-T-to-open-Y section, so
an *equivalent* section is used. With `s` the normalised arc from pedestal (0)
to ridge (1) and `k = 0.55 + 0.45 · (rib arc / 64 m)` a size factor for smaller
shells:

```
h(s) = k · (2.40 → 0.75) m        depth,  linear in s
b(s) = k · (1.60 → 1.10) m        width,  linear in s
A     = κ_A(s) · b h,             κ_A: 0.55 → 0.28
I_z   = κ_I(s) · b h³/12,         κ_I: 0.62 → 0.30      (in the plane of the arch)
I_y   = κ_I(s) · h b³/12,                               (out of plane)
J     = 0.35 (I_z + I_y)
```

`κ_A` and `κ_I` are the "solidity" of the section relative to its enclosing
rectangle: 0.55 / 0.62 stands for the solid T at the base, 0.28 / 0.30 for the
open Y at the ridge, interpolated linearly. **These two ramps are invented.**
The published record describes the transition qualitatively; the numbers here
are a plausible engineering caricature of it, chosen so that material and
bending stiffness both fall towards the ridge, and nothing more. The local
strong axis of every rib element is set to the sphere's radial direction, so
bending is correctly resolved in and out of the shell surface.

**Tile-lid ties.** The tile lids span between neighbouring ribs and are bolted
to both, and that is what gives the shell its transverse stiffness — a fan of
unconnected ribs is not a shell. Each pair of adjacent ribs is therefore tied
at every segment station by one elastic beam-column standing for the lid course
between them: rectangular, 0.16 m thick by the segment length wide. **The
assumption is that the lid course acts as a fully continuous elastic tie in
bending and axial force.** In reality the lids are discrete precast trays on
bolted, mortared joints and the connection is much softer in bending than
modelled. The last course, at the ridge or at the free small-circle edge, is
given a 0.90 m deep section to stand for the ridge / edge beam. The two mirror
half-shells of a main shell **share** their ridge nodes wherever both ribs
reach the ridge, so the pair acts as an arch rather than as two independent
cantilevers; past the taper knee the ribs stop short and the tie becomes a free
edge beam instead.

**Yes — the tile-lid ties carry the transverse stiffness of the shell in this
model, and the modal result depends on them more than on anything else.** A fan
of ribs with no ties is a set of independent arches, not a shell. Measured:
halving the lid-tie thickness from 0.16 m to 0.08 m lengthens T₁ from
**1.377 s to 1.728 s, +25.5 %** (`results.json → lid_tie_sensitivity`). The
real ties are discrete precast trays on bolted, mortared joints, certainly
softer in bending than the continuous elastic beam used here, so the real
structure — if it could be measured — would sit somewhere on the soft side of
the number quoted in §8. That is the single largest uncertainty in the modal
result and it is an assumption, not a measurement.

**The sail taper is a reconstruction, and it changes the shape.** The published
sources give the sphere, the rib spacing and the rise; they do not give the plan
outline of any shell. The taper rule above was fitted by eye against
photographs, and it is the only place in the geometry where a *shape* decision
was made that the sources do not support. It is stated here, in §3 and in
`results.json` so that it can be undone: set `TAPER_DEPTH = 0` in
`build_model.py` and every rib runs to the ridge again.

**Masses.** Rib mass from section area × segment length × 2400 kg/m³. Tile-lid
mass at a flat **100 kg/m²** of shell surface (roughly 35 mm of precast
concrete plus tiles, bedding and joints), lumped to the tie's end nodes.
Podium deck at 600 kg/m². Total model mass **36 500 t**.

**Materials.** E = 34 GPa, ν = 0.20, ρ = 2400 kg/m³ — plausible for the
high-quality 1960s precast concrete used, but no published value was found.
All elements are `elasticBeamColumn`; no cracking, no prestress, no creep, no
construction sequence, no soil.

**Podium.** Deliberately token, so the shells read as sitting on something. A
line-member deck at 12.4 m with transverse support lines at the published 49 m
and 41 m folded-beam spans (the remaining 30 m of the 120 m width is one edge
bay, which is a made-up number), 13 longitudinal grid lines over 183 m, columns
down to sea level, all fixed at the base. It is not a model of the podium.

## 6. Verification actually run

All four checks run inside `build_model.py`; the first three are hard
assertions and the run aborts if any fails. Results are in
`results.json → verification`.

| # | assertion | tolerance | measured | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | every rib node lies on the 75.000 m sphere of its own half-shell | < 1 × 10⁻⁶ m | max deviation **1.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ m** over 2 916 rib nodes | **PASS** |
| 2 | the tallest shell reaches 54.6 m above its springing point | ± 0.05 m | **54.6000 m**, error 0.0000 m | **PASS** |
| 3 | the whole model fits the published 183 × 120 m envelope | ≤ 183.0 × 120.0 m | **183.00 × 120.00 m** | **PASS** |
| 4 | renders are not blank **and the drawn structure fills the frame** — the library's own sheet measure (row-median background, ink bounding box), taken over a horizontal band that excludes the height ruler and the north arrow, so it measures the structure and not the furniture | ink > 3 % **and** the structure's bounding box ≥ 55 % of frame height **and** ≥ 78 % of the band width | `paper/harbour.png` ink 36.8 %, band 90 % × 100 %; `paper/north.png` ink 21.9 %, band 90 % × 98 %; `studio/model.png` ink 20.8 %, band 88 % × 100 %; worst of the twelve is `paper/mode_01.png` at band 75 % × 100 % | **PASS**, 6 images × 2 themes |

The criterion in row 4 was deliberately tightened after the first pass. The
earlier threshold (ink > 1 %, bbox > 25 % / 35 % of the whole canvas) passed
images in which the building filled a third of the frame with empty background
around it, which is not a useful check. The cause was in the camera fit, not in
the model — see §7.

Assertion 1 is the important one: "every shell is cut from one sphere" is the
defining property of the design, and it holds to machine precision because the
geometry is generated from the sphere rather than fitted to it.

## 7. Drawing: what was changed, and does it read as the Opera House?

### The library does this now — every runtime override is deleted

This script used to set four things on the imported vbviz modules at import
time. Since vbviz `eaede95` all four are in the library, and all four overrides
have been **deleted** from `build_model.py`. Nothing under `vbviz/` is patched
and nothing is monkey-patched at run time.

| what the script used to patch | what it does now |
|---|---|
| `vbviz.render.pv.fit_camera = _fill_camera` — the library fitted the eight corners of the bounding box, and a 154 × 116 × 55 m roof lying diagonally in its box filled about a third of the frame | nothing. `fit_camera` fits the **node positions** and *solves* the zoom (it goes in as readily as out), with `fill=` and `lens_mm=` keywords for the callers that need them |
| `palette.TYPE_COLOR.update(...)`, `themes.STUDIO_TYPE.update(...)`, `palette.TYPE_LABEL.update(...)`, `palette.TYPE_ORDER[:0] = [...]` | one `palette.register({...})` call with seven keys, each carrying its paper colour, its studio colour and its legend label. Both themes, the PNG legends and the HTML layer lists follow |
| `pv._overlay` wrapped to cap the furniture dpi, because seven legend rows collided on a 900 px frame | nothing. The library solves the legend row pitch from the drawn text height |
| — (the ground plate used to be a square 1.5 × the largest plan dimension, which ran off this building's canvas, and the ghost was a flat alpha that a shell of thin ribs could not carry) | nothing. The plate is rectangular, plan-sized, 1.15 × the footprint and hard capped at 1.3 ×; the ghost scales its alpha and width with member density |

The colour scheme itself is unchanged: one hue per shell group (**major hall
terracotta / amber, minor hall steel blue / teal, restaurant olive / violet**),
pale tile-lid ties, dark ridge and free-edge beams, near-black pedestal
segments, neutral podium. Rib tubes are drawn at 0.42 × the section depth, so
the solid-T-to-open-Y taper of §4 is visible in the render.

**Effect on the numbers in `results.json`.** Nothing structural moved — the
periods, the mass, the geometry and every geometric verification are
byte-identical to the previous run. Only `verification.renders` moved, because
the camera is now the library's (fill 0.84 and the subject re-centred, against
the override's 0.80 and no re-centring): e.g. `paper/model.png` height fill
0.883 → 0.968, `paper/mode_01.png` 0.805 → 0.751. Every one of the twelve still
passes its threshold.

### The modes page, checked on the page itself

`verify_modes_page()` runs on every run and raises rather than warns. The
framing is now measured **analytically on the written `modes.html`**: every
point the file draws, projected through the camera the file itself carries —
the measurement of vbviz `tests/test_nontower.py` section 10. The ground plate
is the `ground` trace and the structure is everything else, so the two are
measured apart without a second render. The companion shot written with
`ground=False, ghost=False` is **gone**: it took a whole extra render to answer
a question the page's own camera already answers, and it could only see ink,
not the plate corners the camera is fitted to. The grid and the ghost are still
measured on the real page shots (`paper/preview/modes.png`,
`studio/preview/modes.png`), each as its own colour at its own alpha over the
local background, because those are pixel questions.

| check | how it is measured | result |
|---|---|---|
| everything the page draws — the ground plate included — is inside the frame, and the camera is aimed at the drawing rather than past it | the projected margins of every trace on the written page, through that page's own camera: each margin ≥ 0.02 of the canvas, and top vs bottom and left vs right within 0.06 of each other | top **0.171**, bottom **0.171**, left **0.040**, right **0.040** — centred to within 0.000 both ways — **PASS**, both themes |

| the ground grid stays within 1.3 × the plan extent | the plate the library sizes (`style.camera.ground_extent`) against the plan, and the grid's drawn width against the structure's drawn width on the screenshot | plate 1.15 × plan both ways; grid 1.14 × the structure across the frame — **PASS** |
| the undeformed ghost is visible inside the structure band | the ghost colour at the ghost alpha over the local (gradient) background, counted inside the structure's bounding box | paper 1.31 % of the band at contrast 0.175; studio 0.40 % at contrast 0.235 — **PASS** |
| all three shell groups are drawn, and the page says that mode 1 is local | **not** on pixels: modes.html paints every member by its amplitude, so on studio a member at zero amplitude is the ramp's own `#1b3b5a`, within 0.01 of the grid colour, and a minor-hall rib is the same colour as a restaurant rib. Asserted on the page's own geometry payload instead — the segment list must carry every element of all three groups — and the info-box text must carry the local-mode sentence | `rib_major` 1066, `rib_minor` 850, `rib_rest` 216; 5 255 segments = 5 255 elements; the note “mode 1 is a LOCAL mode of the largest sail” is on the page — **PASS** |

**Mode 1 is a local mode, and the page says so.** The three shells stand on
their own pedestals, so the lowest eigenvalue belongs to the softest shell —
the largest sail — and not to any global sway (§8). The info box of
`modes.html` carries that sentence, and `verify_modes_page` asserts it is
there, so a reader cannot meet the animation without the explanation.

There is **no `fill` override any more**: the page is written at the library
default (0.92 on the HTML pages, 0.84 on the PNGs). The earlier `fill = 0.86`
— and the 0.82 before it — were working round a camera that fitted the union
of the nodes and the 183 × 120 m plate but kept *looking* at the centre of the
scene box, which dropped the plate's near corner off the bottom of the page.
Since vbviz `9701d68` the plotly camera aims at the centre of the cloud it
fits, and since `99b754b` the grid is drawn out to the plate edge — the
corners the camera reserves room for — so the default now leaves 0.171 above
the drawing and 0.171 below it instead of 0.171 and 0.190.

**Node count, reconciled.** The page says **2 704 nodes / 5 255 elements**,
`results.json → size` says 2 704 / 5 255, and §5 of this report says 2 704. All
three agree, and `verify_modes_page` asserts the page against the extracted
model on every run, so they cannot drift apart silently.

### The silhouette itself

`harbour.png` now includes the podium, so the sails sit on their base instead of
floating; `north.png` and `west.png` are the roof alone.

My honest judgement, looking at the final images:

* **`north.png` — a reader who knows the building would name it cold.** Two
  clusters of nested pointed sails of falling height, the dark ridge beams
  reading as the spines, the ribs fanning down into black pedestal feet, the two
  small restaurant shells to one side. No caption needed.
* **`harbour.png` — yes, with a second's thought.** The stepped file of shells,
  the mouths open toward the harbour, the ribs converging into the pedestals,
  the whole thing sitting on a podium of the right proportions. It reads as the
  Opera House rather than as generic vaults, but it is not the instant hit that
  the north elevation is.
* **`west.png` and `model.png` — recognisable but not compelling.** From due
  west the sails read as leaning ribbed leaves.

### What I changed after looking at renders and judging them wrong

1. First attempt cut every rib at the ridge with the fan spread over 88°: closed
   rounded humps, croissants not sails. Reduced the fan to −62°…+7°.
2. First attempt put the mouth cut past the apex, so the shells closed over the
   top and lost the pointed arch. Moving the cut to the apex plane produced the
   nested pointed arches that make the north elevation work.
3. First layout put the small side shells directly in front of the big mouths.
   D6/D8 moved outboard to the flanks, the restaurant group to the north-east
   corner.
4. The podium deck sagged 0.96 m because the published 49 m and 41 m support
   lines did not land on the deck grid. Rebuilt the grid from those spans.
5. **The taper (§3, §4).** Without it every rib runs to the ridge, the shell
   holds its full 59 m width all the way back, and the sails read as hand fans.
   Tried three settings and looked at each: a deep taper (0.46 at −20°) made the
   sails stubby, a wide fan with a deep taper (−78°, 0.33) made them into hoods
   again; 0.23 at −25° over a −62° fan narrows the largest sail from 59.1 m at
   the pedestal line to 24.6 m at the tail tip while keeping the lean.
6. Colour and framing, above. This was the single biggest change to how the
   images read.

### What still is not right

* The real sails **lean north** and their mouths are cut by an **inclined**
  glass-wall plane, so the apex overhangs. Mine are upright with a vertical
  mouth. A rigid lean of each half-shell about its pedestal is implemented
  (`LEAN`) but left at 0°: leaning lifts the tail into the air and looked worse.
  An inclined mouth plane through the pedestal cuts almost nothing, because near
  the pedestal every rib rises at the same angle — the real cut plane does not
  pass through the pedestal, and finding where it does pass is not supported by
  the sources.
* The shells are line elements. The real building is a continuous surface, and
  no arrangement of tubes will read as white tile.
* The podium is a token grid, so at close range it reads as scaffolding rather
  than as the great stone base it is.

## 8. Analyses — indicative only

**Gravity** (nodal self-weight, `LoadControl`, 10 steps). Reported for the real
structure: maximum displacement **0.339 m**, at (−12.0, −29.6, 67.0) — the crown
of the largest shell A1, at the apex of its mouth arch. That is a property of
the model, not an artefact: it is the crest of a 59 m span, 54.6 m rise concrete
arch whose ribs taper to 0.75 m deep at the ridge, and it is large because the
model gives the mouth arch no extra stiffening, where the real building has a
heavy edge member there.

**186 nodes are excluded from that maximum**: the tips of every rib that the
fitted taper cuts short of the ridge, plus the outermost rib of each half-shell.
Those are cantilevered free corners created by the reconstruction, not by the
building. Their own maximum is quoted separately as **0.341 m** — with the taper
in place the artefact no longer dominates the result, which it did before
(0.405 m against 0.339 m). The podium deck sags 0.095 m. Both numbers are in
`results.json → gravity`.

**Eigen** (`-genBandArpack`, 6 modes), **for demonstration of the toolchain
only**:

| mode | T (s) | f (Hz) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.377 | 0.726 |
| 2 | 1.176 | 0.851 |
| 3 | 0.969 | 1.032 |
| 4 | 0.888 | 1.127 |
| 5 | 0.844 | 1.185 |
| 6 | 0.782 | 1.279 |

**Mode 1 is a LOCAL mode of the largest sail, and that is physically expected.**
The three shells stand on their own pedestals and are tied to nothing but the
podium, so there is no global mode for this structure to have: the lowest
eigenvalue belongs to whichever shell is softest, which is the largest sail.
The same line is in the info box of `modes.html`, so a reader does not take it
for a display fault.

**These periods are not to be compared with anything.** There is no published
measurement of this building's dynamic behaviour, so there is no benchmark, no
error, and no validation. They are dominated by two assumed quantities: the
equivalent rib sections of §4 and the stiffness of the tile-lid ties. The lid
ties were measured: halving their thickness gives **T₁ = 1.728 s, +25.5 %**. A
quarter of the first period is sitting on one number that nobody published.

---

## 9. What could not be reproduced

1. **Anything dynamic, measured.** No ambient or forced-vibration data for the
   Sydney Opera House is public. No validation is possible.
2. **The true rib cross-sections.** The solid-T-to-open-Y transition is
   described qualitatively; no dimensioned section at any station was found.
   The equivalent-section ramps in §4 are invented.
3. **The rib-to-rib and lid-to-rib connections.** Modelled as continuous
   elastic beams; in reality bolted, mortared, post-tensioned precast joints of
   unknown and certainly lower rotational stiffness.
4. **The shell origins and orientations.** Only the largest shell's crest is
   published. Every other shell's size, station and fan extent is my
   reconstruction (§3).
5. **The plan outline of each shell.** The taper towards the tail is now
   modelled, but as a two-parameter rule fitted by eye to photographs (§3, §4),
   not from a published outline. The inclined glass-wall mouth planes are still
   not reproduced.
6. **The podium.** Two beam spans and a footprint are published; the rest —
   grid, sections, column layout, the folded-beam geometry itself — is invented
   to give the shells something to stand on.
7. **The pedestals.** Modelled as fully fixed points. The real pedestals are
   massive reinforced concrete blocks on the podium structure, with real
   flexibility and real load paths into it.
8. **Prestress, cracking, construction sequence, epoxy-glued precast joints,
   soil–structure interaction, and the glass walls** — none of it is modelled.
9. **The louvre shells C9/C10** are represented by the same main-shell rule at
   small scale; their actual geometry as louvre shells is not reproduced.
