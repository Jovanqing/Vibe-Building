# Lakeside Music Academy — vbviz product set

The building of `ssot/examples/building_003.ssot.json` (project id
`lakeside_music_academy`, the building in `docs/showcase/03_concept_dusk.png`)
brought into the vbviz pipeline: the project's own geometry kernel
`ssot/viz/massing_frame_analysis.massing_to_frame` meshes the massing boxes,
`vbviz.adapters.vb.from_massing_frame` converts the result into a vbviz `Model`,
and `vbviz.export_all` writes the product set into `paper/`, `studio/` and
`studio_white/`. Driver: `build_model.py`. Numbers: `results.json`.

**The rendered set is variant B** — all six massing boxes structural, with the
clock room seated on the tower (§4). Variant A, the source's own structural
definition (four boxes), is the faithful-to-source reference. Variant B0, B
without the seating, is measured on every run because it is the finding that
motivated the reconstruction (§3). Only B is rendered.

Nothing under `~/Desktop/乌镇` was read except the SSOT and the kernel, and
nothing there was written. The variant-B SSOT is a local copy:
`building_003.variant_b.ssot.json`.

## 2. Assumptions

| what | value | where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| mass caliber | floor tributary area × 6750 Pa / 9.81, **self weight excluded**; 267 lumped nodal masses, 3 703.2 t total | `vbviz.adapters.vb.from_massing_frame` (`LOAD_PA = 6750`, `GACC = 9.81`) — the caliber `examples/export_v4.py` uses, so these periods are on the same footing as the v4 set |
| gravity case | the same floor load as vertical nodal loads, 10 equal linear steps | `vb.gravity_state` |
| columns | 0.6 × 0.6 m, A = 0.36 m², I = 0.0108 m⁴ | `massing_frame_analysis._FD_DEFAULT`; the SSOT carries **no** `frame_design` block, so the kernel's starting sections stand — they were never iterated by the design loop for this file |
| beams | 0.3 × 0.6 m | idem |
| bracing | none — no box is a core (`role: core`, `structural.solid`, or `core` in the id) | kernel, core branch |
| material | E = 30 GPa, ν = 0.2, elastic `elasticBeamColumn` throughout | kernel constants |
| self weight | **not** in the model. Since the kernel's 2026-08-02 fix its own `node_gravity` adds RC member self weight at 25 kN/m³; the vbviz adapter's caliber predates that and does not. Every period here is therefore shorter than the kernel's own pipeline would now report, and the two sets must not be mixed. | `massing_frame_analysis.node_gravity` vs `vb._masses` |
| drawing radii | columns 0.30 m (half the section), beams √(0.3·0.6/π) = 0.239 m | adapter |
| member colours | one registered palette key per massing box, applied to that box's **columns**, plus `beam` and `brace` | `register_palette` / `label_by_box`; attribution is by re-meshing each box alone and matching node-coordinate pairs — the kernel says which box made which member, nothing is inferred from geometry. One correction, below. |
| dropped columns are labelled by the space they occupy | 54 of B's columns relabelled | a box in the air has its columns dropped to grade by the kernel; below its own `base_z` those columns are not that box, they are the shaft they stand in. In B the cap's 54 sub-33 m column segments run down the tower's own axes and are drawn as tower columns. Without this the whole tower shaft renders as "clock-room cap". |

### What `massing_to_frame` decided

| decision | consequence here |
|---|---|
| `floor_h = 3.6 m` from `grammar.floor_h`, **one value for the whole building** | the portico's own `grammar.floor_h: 4.8` is ignored: the 5.0 m portico gets `round(5/3.6) = 1` storey, levels at z = 0 and 5.0 |
| `target_bay = 6.0 m`, `ncx = round(W/6)`, `ncy = round(D/6)`, at least 1 | clock tower 6 × 12 m → 2 × 3 column lines; teaching block 32 × 18 m → 6 × 4; rehearsal wing 14 × 14 m → 3 × 3; annex 26 × 10 m → 5 × 3; portico 14 × 7 m → 3 × 2; cap (seated) 6 × 12 m → 2 × 3, the tower's grid exactly. Bays are the footprint divided evenly, so **no two boxes share a grid line unless their footprints divide to the same coordinate** |
| top level snapped to `base_z + H` | the tower's levels are 0, 3.6 … 28.8, **33.0** (last storey 4.2 m) |
| a box with `base_z > 0` drops its columns to grade, **with no beams below `base_z`** | the cap's six legs run the full 33 m. Seated (B) they run down the tower's own axes and are tied to it at grade and at 33.0 m; unseated (B0) they stand free — §3 |
| the dropped legs are spaced `base_z / round(base_z / floor_h)` = 33/9 = 3.667 m | those intermediate nodes do **not** coincide with the tower's 3.6 m levels, so even seated the legs stay a second, parallel set of columns on the same six axes — see §4 |
| floor mass only above `base_z` | the 33 m of leg carries no mass, only the clock room on top |
| `structural.include` read for `profile_tower` only (kernel line 236) | every `massing_box` is meshed whatever the flag says — §5 |
| underground boxes and near-duplicate double skins dropped | neither applies; all six boxes survive |

## 3. The flagpole — the finding that motivated the reconstruction

Variant B0 is the honest reading of the source file with both `include` flags
flipped and nothing else touched. In it, `tower_cap` sits at `base_z = 33` on a
footprint inflated 0.6 m all round (15.4…22.6 × −0.6…12.6 against the tower's
16…22 × 0…12). Because that inflated footprint shares no grid line with the
tower, the kernel's "drop the columns to grade" rule gives the cap **six 33 m
legs of its own, beamless over their whole length**, standing beside the tower
rather than on it: a free-standing flagpole, its own connected component of 66
nodes. Those legs pass straight *through* the teaching block's corner and the
portico without connecting to either.

It is the softest thing in the model and it owned the top of the spectrum:

| | B0 | B |
|---|---|---|
| pieces | 5 | 4 |
| T1 | 2.1913 s — the cap's legs | 1.0869 s — the tower with the clock room on it |
| T2 | 2.1426 s — the cap's legs | 0.9644 s — the same piece, other direction |
| T3 | 1.1324 s | 0.7814 s — the teaching block |

`modes.html` in B0 animates six bare legs swaying beside a stationary building,
and the headline period belongs to nothing anybody designed. That is why B0 is
computed and reported but not rendered.

## 4. The reconstruction: the clock room sits on the tower

Made in the local copy `building_003.variant_b.ssot.json`, **not** in the kernel
and not in the source:

| | before (source) | after (variant B) |
|---|---|---|
| `tower_cap.geometry.footprint` | `[15.4, −0.6, 22.6, 12.6]` | `[16.0, 0.0, 22.0, 12.0]` — the clock tower's own footprint |
| `base_z` | 33.0 m | 33.0 m (unchanged) |
| `height` | 3.5 m | 3.5 m (unchanged) |

**Why:** the source inflates the cap 0.6 m all round because the cap is
render-only geometry — a cornice overhang, and `structural.include: false` says
as much. For a structural model the clock room sits on the tower. With the
footprints equal the cap is meshed on the tower's own 2 × 3 grid, and its
columns land on the tower's roof nodes: the two boxes now share **12 nodes**,
six at the roof (z = 33.0) and six at grade, so the clock room becomes one more
storey of the tower instead of a neighbour.

**What it changed:** 386 → 374 nodes, 713 → 706 elements, 5 → 4 pieces,
3 719.1 → 3 703.2 t (the clock room's plan shrank from 95 to 72 m²), and
T1 from 2.1913 s (a flagpole) to 1.0869 s (the tower).

**What it did not fix, and is not hidden.** The kernel spaces a dropped leg at
`base_z / round(base_z / floor_h)` = 33/9 = 3.667 m, while the tower's own
levels are at 3.6 m. The legs therefore share only grade and the roof with the
tower, and the tower shaft carries **two parallel sets of six columns** — the
tower's 54 segments and the cap's 54 — tied to each other only at their ends.
The shaft's column area is effectively doubled there. That is a kernel meshing
rule meeting a 3.6 m storey that does not divide 33.0 m, and no edit to the
local copy removes it while `base_z` stays at 33.0. It is stated rather than
patched, and it is a small error in one piece's stiffness where the flagpole was
a spurious global mode. (T1 still *rises* from A's 1.0057 s to 1.0869 s: the
clock room's mass at 36.5 m outweighs the doubled shaft.)

In the drawings those 54 duplicate segments are drawn as clock-tower columns,
because that is the volume they occupy; `results.json` records the count as
`variants.B.dropped_columns_relabelled`.

## 5. Connectivity — four pieces

The kernel meshes each box independently and boxes join only where grid nodes
coincide. Counted on variant B's frame (union-find over every column, beam and
brace):

| component | nodes | z range | boxes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 168 | 0 – 21.6 m | `main_teaching` |
| 2 | 125 | 0 – 36.5 m | `clock_tower` + `entrance_portico` + `tower_cap` |
| 3 | 45 | 0 – 7.2 m | `service_annex` |
| 4 | 36 | 0 – 10.8 m | `rehearsal_wing` |

Shared nodes: `clock_tower` + `tower_cap` share 12 (six at grade, six at the
roof, z = 33.0) — the reconstruction of §4. `entrance_portico` shares exactly
one node with the tower, (22.0, 12.0, 0.0), which is a **ground** node and
therefore fixed anyway: the portico is tied to the tower through the foundation,
not through the superstructure, and it does not move in modes 1 and 2.

Everywhere else the grids miss each other, and **nothing is forced**:

* the **tower stands free of the teaching block** — the tower's grid lines are
  x ∈ {16, 22}, y ∈ {0, 6, 12}; the block's are x ∈ {8, 14.4, 20.8, 27.2, 33.6,
  40}, y ∈ {12, 18, 24, 30}. They share the line y = 12 but no x on it. This is
  what the source kernel does with the source footprints and it is left alone.
* `rehearsal_wing` abuts the block along x = 40, but its y values (14, 21, 28)
  miss the block's (12, 18, 24, 30).
* `service_annex` abuts along y = 30, and its x values (10, 16.5, 23, 29.5, 36)
  miss the block's.

Variant A is the same picture with four components — one per box, no shared node
at all.

## 6. What the include flip changed

`entrance_portico` and `tower_cap` carry `structural.include: false` in the
source: the original project never modelled them structurally. The local copy
flips both to `true`.

**The flip is a statement of intent, not a lever on this kernel.**
`massing_to_frame` tests `structural.include` for `profile_tower` elements only
(line 236); the `massing_box` loop below it tests nothing and meshes every box.
So passing the flipped copy to the kernel gives the same frame as passing the
untouched source. Variant A is therefore produced the only way it can be — by
dropping the `include: false` boxes from the element list before the call — and
B0 is what the kernel does with the file as it stands.

Reported, not patched. If the benchmark wants `structural.include` to mean
something for massing boxes, the change belongs in
`ssot/viz/massing_frame_analysis.py` around line 242, one condition, and it
would change the frame of every instance in the library whose boxes carry the
flag.

What the two extra boxes add to the rendered model: +65 nodes, +80 elements,
+117.0 t, +3.5 m of height, and a first period 8 % longer than A's. What they
add visually is the colonnade and the clock room — the two things that make the
render recognisable — which is why B is the set that ships.

not comparable to a frame model except where the quantity is the same.** The
elements say so themselves: each box carries `structural.fe_type: "shell"` with
a thickness of 0.25–0.35 m. A 0.30 m shell wrapping a 32 × 18 m block and a
0.35 m shell wrapping the tower is a *tube*; what is modelled here is a bare
0.6 × 0.6 m column grid with 0.3 × 0.6 m beams and no walls, no slabs and no
cores. Beside it:

| quantity | saved (Abaqus shell) | here (frame) | comparable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | 0.2371 s | 1.0057 s (A) / 1.0869 s (B) | **no** — different structure, different stiffness, different mass caliber. The ratio is what a shell tube versus a bare frame looks like, nothing more. |
| max static displacement | 0.05211 m | 0.00178 m | **no** — and not the same load case: the saved one is the SSOT's `dead` self-weight case on a shell, ours is 6750 Pa of floor load on a frame with no self weight. |
| von Mises stress, utilisation | 15.6 MPa, 3.262 | — | **not produced** — a line-element model has no von Mises field; vbviz draws N, Vy and Mz per member instead. |
| storey drifts (4 values) | 2.46e-4 … 1.13e-4 | — | **not produced** here; four storey values for a 9-storey tower plus a 6-storey block is itself a shell-model discretisation artefact. |
| effective mass ratio X | 0.3555 over 8 modes | — | **no** — and in our frame low participation has a different cause: four independent pieces, each with its own modes, so no single mode carries much of the total mass. |

No validation is claimed. Nothing above is offered as a check on the saved
values, and the saved values are not a check on anything here.

## 9. Do the pictures read as the building?

`studio/model.png` and `studio_white/model.png`, against
`docs/showcase/03_concept_dusk.png`:

**Yes — tower, blocks and portico all read, and the clock room now sits on the
tower.** The slender clock tower stands at the left, alone, climbing past
everything else; its shaft is one orange 2 × 3 column bundle from grade to
33.0 m and the pink clock room is the storey on top of it, on the same
footprint, ending at 36.5 m — the render's silhouette. The long 6-storey
teaching block fills the centre at 21.6 m on its regular 6 × 4 bay grid; the
3-storey rehearsal wing steps down to the right at 10.8 m; the 2-storey service
annex sits behind the block (violet, half hidden, as a low volume behind a
taller one does in a 3/4 view); and the entrance portico is a row of six
free-standing yellow columns 5 m tall at the front left with its own beam grid
on top and nothing above it — a colonnade, as in the render. The silhouette
steps 36.5 → 21.6 → 10.8 → 7.2 → 5.0 m from left to right, which is the render's
profile.

`studio_white/preview/modes.png` (mode 1, T = 1.087 s): the amplitude ramp
lights the **whole clock tower**, base to clock room, brass at the top and
falling to the dark blue of zero at grade, with the clock room riding on the
shaft; every other piece is at the zero colour. The first mode is the tower
swaying, not a detached piece. The page's info box reads "374 nodes / 706
elements · 4 disconnected pieces · T1 = 1.087 s · 706 of 706 members moving".

Two honest differences from the render, unchanged by any of this:

* no cladding, no glazing, no clock face, no cornice: vbviz draws line members
  and translucent floor plates only (`docs/PRODUCTS.md` §3). The render's
  character comes largely from its facade; what the pictures share with it is
  the massing, the storey heights and the bay rhythm.
* the four pieces are four pieces. The tower is drawn beside the teaching block
  and not tied to it, because that is what the kernel builds (§5).

`paper/model.png` reads the same on a white page with the mineral palette.
