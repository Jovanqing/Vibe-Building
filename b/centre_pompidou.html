# Centre Georges Pompidou, Paris — OpenSeesPy structural model

Piano + Rogers with Ove Arup & Partners (Peter Rice), 1971–1977.
Build script: `build_model.py`. Results: `results.json`. Renders: `paper/`, `studio/`.

---

## 0. This is a demonstration, not a validation

**No measured dynamic data for the Centre Pompidou exists in any source reached.**
There is no published ambient-vibration survey, no forced-vibration test, no
instrumented earthquake record. There is therefore nothing to compare a computed
period against, and **no validation is possible and none is claimed**. Every
period in section 6 is *indicative only* — it tells you what this particular set
of assumptions produces, and nothing about the building.

What this exercise does demonstrate is that the toolchain (OpenSeesPy → vbviz)
handles a large orthogonal exposed steel frame: 8 853 elements, real trusses
rather than smeared beams, a gravity solve and an eigen solve, and a render in
which the structural system is legible.

## 2. The count consistency check — the model agrees

The check offered in the brief was verified independently in the script
(`N_LEVELS_CHECK`, asserted at run time):

* 28 columns × 6 gerberette levels = **168** = the published gerberette count. ✔
* 84 main beams ÷ 14 column lines = **6** levels. ✔

So the frame has **six gerberette-borne truss levels, not seven**. The model
agrees, and it goes one step further by saying where the seventh level goes:

* Level 1 is the ground/Piazza floor at ±0.00, on grade, needing no gerberette.
* Levels 2–7 are the six suspended decks at +6, +12, +18, +24, +30, +36 m, each
  on its own pair of gerberettes per column line → **6 × 28 = 168 gerberettes**
  and **6 × 14 = 84 main-beam trusses**, both exactly the published figures.
* The **roof at +42 m** (the published Beaubourg height) is framed by a 14th set
  of trusses that seats **directly on the column heads**, not on gerberettes.
  This is the structural reason there are six gerberette levels and not seven,
  and it is why the model's total truss count is 98 while the published main-beam
  count is 84.

A second published number falls out of the same scheme. 166 × 60 × 7 = 69 720 m²,
which is **less** than the published 103 305 m². The published area must therefore
include the below-grade levels; 166 × 60 × 10 = 99 600 m² matches to within 4 %,
consistent with Pompidou's three basement levels. This is reported in
`results.json` as an observation, not asserted.

## 3. The truss dimension conflict — resolved

Quoted spans: 48 m, 51 m, 44.8 m. Quoted depths: 2.85 m, 4.80 m.

### Chosen set

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Truss span, gerberette seat to gerberette seat | **44.8 m** |
| Main beam member length | **45.0 m** (published) |
| Truss depth | **2.85 m** |
| Column grid across the width | **48.0 m** |
| Gerberette inner (short) arm | 1.6 m |
| Gerberette outer (long) arm | 6.4 m (1.6 + 6.4 = the published 8 m) |
| Outer envelope width | **60.8 m** vs the published 60 m (+1.3 %) |
| Floor to floor | 6.0 m |

### Why this set and not another

The gerberette is published as 8 m long and rocks on the column, so two
identities are forced by the geometry:

```
column grid across the width = truss span + 2 x (inner arm)
outer envelope width         = truss span + 2 x 8 m
```

With a 44.8 m span and a 1.6/6.4 m split of the 8 m casting:

* 44.8 + 2 × 1.6 = **48.0 m** → the "48 m" quote is the **column grid**, and it
  comes out exact.
* 44.8 + 2 × 8.0 = **60.8 m** → the published 60 m envelope, 1.3 % over.
* The published **45 m** main beam is the member length of a 44.8 m span truss.

Four independent published numbers — 8 m gerberette, 45 m beam, 60 m width, and
the "48 m" quote — all fall out of one geometry. That is why this set was chosen.

### Rejected, with reasons

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| **Span 51 m** | 51 + 2 × 8 = 67 m, **12 % wider** than the published 60 m envelope. 51 m × 4.80 m are the figures of the *rejected competition scheme*, not of the built building. |
| **Span 48 m read as the truss span** | 48 + 16 = 64 m envelope (**6.7 % over**), and a 48 m span cannot produce a 45 m main beam. 48 m is instead reproduced exactly as the column grid. |
| **Depth 4.80 m** | Leaves 1.2 m under a 6.0 m floor-to-floor, in a building whose services are all in the 8 m outer zone rather than in the ceiling; it also belongs to the 51 m competition truss. |

The 2.85 m depth also sets where the gerberette sits: the deck is on the truss
**top** chord, so the gerberette and the bottom chord are 2.85 m below deck level
(at +3.15, +9.15 … +33.15 m).

## 5. The escalator: the brief's "zig-zag" does not close

The brief describes the tube as "ten runs of 16.7 m each, about 45 m in total,
zig-zagging up the facade". The arithmetic says otherwise, and the model follows
the arithmetic:

* 10 runs × 16.7 m = **167 m of run length**, so "about 45 m in total" cannot be
  a length; it is the **total rise**, which matches the 45.5 m Piazza height.
* 45 m of rise over 10 runs is 4.5 m per run, so each 16.7 m run advances
  √(16.7² − 4.5²) = **16.08 m horizontally**.
* 10 × 16.08 m = **160.8 m** — essentially the whole 166.4 m facade.

Runs that reversed direction could not consume 160 m of a 166 m facade. So the
runs all advance the same way: **one continuous diagonal**, which is what the
photographs of the Piazza facade show.

The model builds that, and it is now **entirely inside the published footprint**:
the landings between runs are 0.5 m, so the tube is 160.8 + 9 × 0.5 = 165.3 m
long against a 166.4 m facade, leaving 0.54 m at each end. It starts at Piazza
grade (−3.50 m) at the west end and finishes at +41.5 m, 0.5 m under the +42 m
roof. All four of those numbers are asserted (section 6.1).

### The tube is a wireframe, not a solid

It used to be one line of beam elements drawn at a 1.6 m radius: a solid grey
cylinder 3.2 m across and 165 m long, lying diagonally over the Piazza facade.
In the three-quarter view that reads as a **rogue inclined column driven through
the building**, which is not what the chenille is. The real object is a glass
tube on a steel frame, so it is now modelled as one:

| | value | status |
|---|---|---|
| tube diameter | 3.2 m | **assumed** — not published, scaled off photographs against the 12.8 m bay |
| ring spacing along the axis | 4.0 m (10 runs × 4 rings + a ring at each landing + a ring at the head = **50 rings**) | assumed |
| longitudinal members | **8**, one per ring point | assumed |
| member section | CHS 168.3 × 8 (A = 0.0040 m²) | assumed |
| axis stand-off from the tie-rod plane | **3.1 m**, so the tube surface clears the facade plane by 1.5 m | assumed |
| brackets | four members per station (two tube points × the two nearest column levels), every third ring ≈ every 12 m — **68 members** | assumed |
| total | **860 line elements**, 79 t of model steel | — |

The bracket is triangulated for a reason found by measurement, not by taste: a
single tie per station leaves the tube free to **roll** about it (2.5 m of sag
under its own weight), and a single parallel line of ties leaves it swinging
like a **pendulum** (1.3 m). Two tube points tied to two column levels each
gives 0.041 m, less than the frame's own 0.117 m.

**The assertion.** `escalator()` walks the 20 stations of the tube axis and
asserts that none of them lies inside the building envelope
(−83.2 ≤ x ≤ 83.2, |y| ≤ 30.4, −7.0 ≤ z ≤ 42.0), and that the tube *surface*
clears the facade plane. Both are in `results.json → verification`; the axis
runs at y = −33.5 m throughout and the nearest tube node is at y = −31.9 m,
1.5 m outboard of the tie-rod plane. The only part of the tube that is not
inside the published envelope in *z* is the top ring, whose crown reaches
+43.04 m against the +42 m roof — the axis head is at +41.5 m, fixed by the
published 45 m rise from Piazza grade, and the tube has a radius.

### 6.2 Gravity

Maximum downward displacement **0.117 m**, at truss mid-span: L/382 on the 44.8 m
span. Sane for a 2.85 m deep truss at 7.5 kPa. The escalator tube's own worst
sag is **0.041 m**, i.e. it hangs off the frame rather than the other way round.

#### The escalator moved these periods, and by how much

Re-modelling the escalator as a wireframe tube is the only change to the model
since the previous run. It is not a small change to the periods, and it is not
hidden:

| Mode | before (solid cylinder) | after (wireframe tube) | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, longitudinal X | 1.833 s | **2.131 s** | **+16.3 %** |
| 2, transverse Y | 1.278 s | 1.279 s | +0.1 % |
| 3, torsion | 1.136 s | 1.158 s | +1.9 % |
| 4, longitudinal 2nd | 0.990 s | 1.138 s | +15.0 % |
| 5, longitudinal 3rd | 0.641 s | 0.698 s | +9.0 % |
| 6 | 0.581 s | 0.625 s | +7.6 % |
| 7 | 0.580 s | 0.581 s | +0.1 % |
| 8 | 0.578 s | 0.580 s | +0.3 % |

The pattern says exactly what happened. **Only the longitudinal modes moved.**
The old escalator was one continuous 165 m line of beam elements with
A = 0.050 m² and I = 0.060 m⁴, running diagonally across the whole Piazza
facade and tied to the frame at 39 points: a 165 m diagonal brace that nobody
intended to model. Taking it out removes a spurious longitudinal stiffening, so
T₁ lengthens 16 %; the transverse period, which the escalator never braced, is
unchanged to 0.1 %.

The new tube is the honest object: light members, and the axial stiffness of
eight 168 mm CHS spread round a 3.2 m circle rather than one notional
0.05 m² spine. **The new numbers are the better ones**, and the old T₁ = 1.833 s
should be read as having been 16 % too stiff for a reason that had nothing to
do with the building.

**There is nothing to compare these against.** For context only: an empirical
code formula for a 42 m steel moment frame (0.05 H^0.75) gives ≈ 0.83 s, so the
model is roughly twice as flexible as a generic steel building of this height.
That is directionally plausible for an exposed frame whose lateral system is
28 slender 800 mm columns plus a braced facade plane whose chords are 200 mm
rods — but it is a plausibility remark, not a validation, and it could equally
be an artefact of the assumed sections. Longitudinal being softer than transverse
is governed by the tie-rod area, which is published (200 mm) and small.

## 7. Does the render read as the Pompidou? — my own judgement

I looked at `paper/piazza_facade.png`, `paper/view_gable.png`, `paper/model.png`
and `studio/piazza_facade.png` with the Read tool, after each round of changes.

### The honest answer

**The gable view and the 3/4 view read as the Centre Pompidou to someone who
knows it. The Piazza facade reads as it too, but for a different reason, and one
element does not survive that view.**

* `view_gable.png` is the one that settles it. Purple gerberettes cantilever out
  of the red columns on both sides at six levels; the short inner arm carries the
  end of the 44.8 m truss, the long outer arm reaches out to a teal tie rod that
  drops the full height to the foundation; blue X-bracing fills the outer plane
  behind them, and the truss field behind that is quiet grey. That is the
  Pompidou cross-section and nothing else looks like it.
* `piazza_facade.png` reads as the building because of the two things that carry
  that elevation in every photograph: **an X in every one of the 13 bays over all
  six storeys**, and **the escalator tube climbing the whole facade** from grade
  at one end to the top at the other. The seven floors read as stacked bands
  behind the bracing.
* `model.png` shows all of it at once, with the legend.

**The escalator, looked at again after re-modelling it.** I opened
`paper/model.png` (the three-quarter view, azimuth −55°, 16° up) and
`paper/piazza_facade.png` (straight on, 300 mm), and then `studio/model.png`.
My judgement: **it now reads as a tube on the facade, and it did not before.**
On the elevation the ring-and-longitudinal lattice is unmistakably a tube —
you can count the rings, you can see the cross-section of it, and it sits in
front of the blue X-bracing with the bracing visible *through* it, which is
what glass does and what a solid grey cylinder cannot do. On the three-quarter
view the same thing holds: the tube's near and far walls are both drawn, so the
eye reads a hollow object standing off the facade rather than a member of the
frame. The specific failure it had before — a heavy grey diagonal that looked
like a rogue inclined column boring through the building — is gone, because a
transparent lattice cannot be mistaken for a column. What is still not right:
the tube is the palest thing on the paper page, and against a white background
its far side almost disappears at small reproduction sizes; on the studio page,
where it silhouettes against navy, it is clearly the better of the two.

**Restraint did not cost legibility — it improved it.** Restricting colour to
four accents on a grey field makes the gerberettes read *better*, not worse:
they are now the only purple in the image instead of one saturated hue among
nine, so the eye lands on the rocking bracket first, which is the correct
hierarchy for this building. The one thing that got quieter and should not have
was the studio bracing, and that was corrected (see below). What is genuinely
lost is the chord-versus-web distinction inside the trusses: at 0.46 and 0.64
value they now read as one grey lattice. The truss still reads as a truss,
because the geometry does that, so this is an acceptable price.

**What does not survive the Piazza elevation: the gerberettes.** Seen straight on
they are behind the bracing plane and end-on, so they are a row of purple marks.
That is geometrically correct — you do not see a gerberette in a straight-on
elevation of the real building either — but it means the iconic view alone does
not carry the building's defining component, and the gable view has to.

**Where it still falls short of a photograph** (i.e. what is missing, not what is
wrong): no cladding, no glazing, no coloured services in the outer zone, no
ground, no context. A structural model of an exposed frame gets closer to the
photograph than most, but the Pompidou's reading also depends on the blue air
ducts and the red circulation, which are not structure and are not modelled.

### What I changed, and why

Five rounds. The first four renders were not good enough and are not what is in
the folder.

1. **Framing.** vbviz's `fit_camera` used to only ever zoom *out*: it started at
   1.12 and multiplied by 0.88 until the model box was inside the frame. On a
   tower that is enough because `reset_camera` on a tall box already fills a
   portrait frame. A 166 × 60 × 49 m slab filled about a third, and nothing
   pulled it back in. This script carried a replacement installed with `setattr`
   on the live module. **That patch is now deleted**: since vbviz `eaede95` the
   library's own `fit_camera` fits the node cloud and *solves* the zoom, and
   takes `fill=` and `lens_mm=` keywords, which is what the four views pass.
   Measured fill on the written image is **0.84–0.89** across the four views
   against **0.63** before any of this.
2. **Near-orthographic elevations.** The house camera is a 60 mm lens at 2.4 model
   diagonals, which on a 166 m facade makes the near end visibly larger than the
   far end. The three elevations are shot at 200–300 mm, so both ends of the
   facade come out the same size — which is what an elevation has to do.
   `piazza_facade.png` is the dedicated iconic view: straight on, 1.5° above the
   horizon, 300 mm, filling 0.86 of a 2600 × 1000 frame, in both themes.
3. **Escalator inside the envelope, and then not a solid at all.** It previously
   used 1.2 m landings, which made the tube 171.6 m long on a 166.4 m facade, so
   it overhung both ends and floated past the frame at the lower left. Landings
   cut to 0.5 m; the tube now starts at Piazza grade at the west end and finishes
   0.5 m under the roof at the east end, with both endpoints asserted inside the
   published footprint. It was still drawn as a **solid 3.2 m cylinder**, and in
   the three-quarter view that read as an inclined column driven through the
   building. It is now a **wireframe tube** — 50 rings of 8 at 4.0 m spacing,
   8 longitudinals, triangulated brackets — in the lightest neutral of each
   theme, drawn thin (§5). See §7 for what that did to the drawing and §6.3 for
   what it did to the periods.
4. **Palette: four accents and a neutral field.** Two steps here, and the first
   was wrong. vbviz's palette keys are generic and its two themes are variations
   on warm grey (paper) and teal (studio), which buried the gerberettes and the
   tie rods — the whole point of this building — in the floor framing behind
   them. The first fix gave all nine member types their own saturated hue. That
   solved legibility and created a new problem: nine saturated colours read as a
   finite-element debug view, not as a figure. The version in the folder is the
   second fix, hierarchy instead of more hues:

   | Member | paper | studio | role |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | column, 800 mm spun steel | red, S 0.74 | orange, S 0.74 | **accent** |
   | tie rod, 200 mm | teal, S 0.90 | green-teal, S 0.77 | **accent** |
   | gerberette | purple, S 0.61 | violet, S 0.59 | **accent** |
   | facade cross bracing | steel blue, S 0.36 | sky blue, S 0.56 | **accent**, the quiet fourth |
   | escalator tube | grey V 0.88 (lightest) | grey V 0.95 (lightest) | neutral field |
   | truss chord | grey V 0.46 (darkest) | grey V 0.80 | neutral field |
   | truss web | grey V 0.64 | grey V 0.64 | neutral field |
   | longitudinal beam | grey V 0.71 | grey V 0.57 | neutral field |
   | secondary floor beam | grey V 0.82 | grey V 0.49 (darkest) | neutral field |

   The three components that identify this building keep saturated, well
   separated colour. The bracing takes a quieter fourth tone, because it carries
   the Piazza elevation. Everything else drops to steel neutrals separated by
   **value only**, and stays separable in the legend by label. The escalator
   takes the extreme of the value range in each theme — darkest on the white
   page, lightest on the navy — so it still silhouettes.

   `assert_palette()` enforces the restraint rather than leaving it to taste:
   every field member's saturation must be **≤ 0.12**, every accent's **≥ 0.30**,
   no two accent hues closer than **0.10** of the wheel, and no two field greys
   within **0.05** of value. Measured: accent hue gap **0.105 (paper) / 0.102
   (studio)**, field value gap **0.067 / 0.078**.

   One theme-specific correction the restraint forced: on navy, a low-saturation
   steel-blue bracing fell straight into the grey field and the Piazza elevation
   lost the thing that carries it. The studio accents therefore run hotter than
   the paper ones while keeping the same ordering — column and tie rod loudest,
   gerberette next, bracing the quiet fourth.

5. **Legend.** vbviz sizes its furniture as `dpi/200`, so nine legend rows
   overprinted each other on a landscape frame, and the generic keys captioned
   the gerberettes "outrigger". Fixed by rendering at `dpi=150` (same pixel
   count, smaller furniture) and by updating `palette.TYPE_LABEL` at run time.
   The legend is now carried by `model.png` only: on an elevation filling 86 % of
   the frame it would sit on top of the building, and with it off the measured
   colour fractions below are the structure's, not the swatches'.

Nothing under `vbviz/` was edited. All five are `setattr`/`dict.update` on the
imported modules inside `install_render_overrides()`.

### The assertions on the written images

Three per view, all measured on the PNG that was written, none by eye:

* **not blank, and filled**: the background is taken from the image *border*
  (once the structure fills the frame the median *is* the structure and a
  median-based threshold inverts), vbviz's furniture rectangles are masked out,
  and the structure's bounding box must span at least **0.78** of the frame on
  its long side.
* **all four accent hues are on the page**: matched on **hue**, not RGB distance
  — the renderer multiplies a member colour by a light intensity, which moves the
  RGB a long way and the hue not at all. The five field greys are the same hue as
  each other by design, so they are measured as one mask (unsaturated, and
  neither the white page nor the navy ground); separating them is the legend's
  job, by label.
* **the accents and the neutral field are both present in quantity**, i.e. the
  signature components have not been swallowed by the deck. Each must cover more
  than 0.4 % of the image.

The theme's own background is masked out of the hue match. It has to be: the
studio navy sits within 0.03 of the bracing's hue, and without the mask the empty
background alone reported as 80 % bracing — a check that would have passed on a
blank page.

## 8. Honest list of what could not be determined

1. **Any measured dynamic property of the building.** No period, no frequency, no
   damping, no mode shape. This is why no validation is offered.
2. **Column wall thickness.** Assumed 40 mm. The model's lateral stiffness scales
   almost linearly with it and the periods with its square root.
3. **The gerberette bearing.** Pin diameter, bearing radius and the eccentricity
   of the bearing from the column axis are all unpublished. Modelled rigid, which
   is the wrong direction: the real frame is softer than this model.
4. **The gerberette casting profile.** Only its length and mass are published.
   Depth and width were scaled off photographs; the taper is not modelled.
5. **Any member schedule.** Truss chords, webs, facade braces, longitudinal and
   secondary beams are all sized by judgement.
6. **Design floor load and the deck build-up.** Assumed 7.5 kPa.
7. **The escalator tube diameter and its structural section.**
8. **Where the missing 5 290 t of the published 15 000 t sits.** Plausibly
   cladding support, service steel, connections, basement and escalator
   machinery, but no breakdown is published, so the 65 % figure is reported and
   not reconciled.
9. **The basement.** Three levels are known to exist; no framing, no depth, no
   retaining structure is published. Represented by a horizontal restraint at
   grade.
10. **Whether the tie rods are pre-tensioned in the model.** They are not — the
    published 200 mm diameter is used, but no pre-tension force is published, so
    none is applied.
11. **The 60 m envelope to 0.8 m.** The model's structural width is 60.8 m; the
    published 60 m is presumably a rounded external dimension, but which plane it
    is measured to is not stated.
